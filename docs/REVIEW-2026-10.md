# Review of the new UI and what the old one left (October 2026)

The new UI (src/view/, Preact and signals) became the game's only UI in #351, and the old one went in #463. Stage D
was built quickly, by several sessions, in many stacked pull requests, then cut back to a 1:1 port (docs/ui/PLAN.md,
docs/ui/port-audit.md). This page is the review done before the owner's long playtest: what is left of the old UI,
how the new one is built, and what to tidy. **Nothing here changes what the player sees or does.** The bugs found
are GitHub issues, not fixes on the side.

How it was done, on main at `57b64c3`:

- **Read:** the code of src/view/, src/host.ts, src/screen.ts, src/nullscreen.ts, src/game.ts, src/mirror.ts,
  src/context.ts, src/session.ts, src/dom.ts and src/prototypes.ts, with the tests, the harness and the docs.
- **Ran:**
  - `npm run fallow:report`, `npx fallow dead-code` and `npm run gate` (all green);
  - fallow again twice: without main.ts's `?harness` module list, then with only the game's own entries (no tests);
  - the linter with each file taken off each exemption list in turn;
  - `npm run test:coverage` against the floors;
  - two Playwright probes in Chromium: the map canvas's size, and `drawImage` on a broken image.
- **Checked:** every finding below was checked against the code at the line it names. The bugs were traced through the
  code, not played. Where a claim is from reading only and not measured, it says so.

Severities: **bug** (wrong behaviour a player or a tool can hit), **risk** (fragile, slow or leaky, nothing wrong
yet), **cleanup** (no effect on behaviour). The last column says where each finding goes:

- **2a** to **2g**: the clean-up pull requests of step 2 (at the end).
- **issue**: a GitHub issue.
- **proposal**: an architecture proposal for the owner.
- **left**: not worth its change, or the owner's call.

### What still reads the old objects

`GameMap`, `Unit`, `Player`, `Hex`, `MapSelection` and the mirror (src/mirror.ts) are no longer read by any UI: the
linter keeps src/view to the core, the host and the screen interface. Their readers now:

- **The game host** (src/host.ts) runs on them. Who plays a side is `Player.type` and `Player.handler`
  (src/host.ts:172-190, :708-714). A program's turn is `currentPlayer.handler.getAction()` (:436). The campaign
  player is a `Player` copied between scenarios (:204-213). The move range of an AI move comes from
  `GameRules.getMoveRange(map, unit)` (:673).
- **The screen interface** passes old objects to the screen: `move(unit: Unit, to: Cell, ...)` and
  `attack(attacker: Unit, target: Unit, ...)` (src/screen.ts:53-55). Both screens read only `.id`, `.row` and
  `.col` (src/view/screen.tsx:119-126, src/nullscreen.ts:36-45). See T6.
- **The save** (src/savegame.ts, src/state.ts), the AI's old driver, the scenario loader and the campaign, through
  `engineContext` (src/context.ts).
- **The harness** (harness/lib/harness.js snapshots the objects), **the contracts** (test/contracts/*.js) and
  `test/harness-surface.test.ts`, which lists the 24 names they use.
- **The CLI:** only cli/page.ts (`game.newScenario`) and cli/fightlog.ts. The rest of cli/ reads the core's
  `World` (cli/known.ts).
- **Who reads the objects only for the old UI:** `MapSelection` (src/selection.ts: `isMove`, `isAttack`, `after`,
  `reselect`, `landing`) is now read only by the contracts, the harness and test/gameturn.test.ts. Its header says
  so. `GameMap.currentUnit` is the same. The mirror's header (src/mirror.ts:1-23) still says "the objects hold what
  the old UI drew", "the UI compares units with ===", "what the screen writes into them" (2c corrects the comment).
- **game.ts, context.ts, session.ts:** small and used. Game's members are the harness's and the tests' handle on the
  host. 22 of its members are used by harness/ and test/contracts/; `onCampaignLoadFinished` is not (L6).

What could go, and at what cost, is architecture proposals 1 and 2 at the end.

## 2. The new UI's architecture (src/view/)

### The store

src/view/store.ts:58-489 is one closure of about 29 signals, 38 computeds and a `busy` flag. Four sub-stores
(shop, new game, settings, save and load) are spread into the same object, which exposes about 105 members. It holds:

- the world, the selection and the command dispatch;
- the status bar's cells;
- the map's pointer and layers;
- the step's beats;
- the turn reports and the dossier;
- the unit card and its context menu;
- End turn's question;
- the messages;
- the start menu;
- the shortcuts.

The seams are visible in its comments. `createShopState` already shows the pattern: a small core (`world`,
`selected`, `select`, `dispatch`, `refresh`) passed to each part. A split is proposal 3.

### The canvas map

What triggers a draw: one `effect` (map.tsx:88-93) over 15 signals:

- `world`, `units`, `selection`, `path`, `scale`, `fog`, `support`, `plaques`;
- `overview`, `deployment`, `ghost`, `hexGrid`, `glance`, `results`, `motion`.

The window's resize, the scroll, each image's load and `fonts.ready` also trigger one. `requestAnimationFrame` merges
them into one draw a frame (map.tsx:127-134). Every draw is a full redraw (mapdraw.ts:355-390), in this order:

- the terrain image;
- the grid and the fog, kept to the hexes on screen;
- the selection, then every plaque and unit sprite;
- every marker and chip.

How often (from the code, not measured):

- **Pointer moves.** `hoverHex` drops a move within the same hex (store.ts:460-464). A new hex redraws only when the
  path, ghost, support or glance changes: with a unit selected, every hex inside its move area or on a target.
- **A step's beats.** A move writes a new `Motion` every 30 ms (5 with quick animation), a shot every 150 (50) ms
  (player.ts:24-26): 33 full redraws a second during a move.
- **An idle `refresh()`** is free: `rulesWorld` returns the cached world when nothing changed (mirror.ts:197-202).

## 6. Performance

The canvas findings F1-F11 above are the hot paths. Outside the canvas:

- **The world.** It is built once per step, and the store's computeds that walk the whole map on a world change are
  `units`, `fog`, `plaques` and `selection`: about rows × cols each. Fine at a step's rate.
- **The beat player.** It schedules every frame of a beat at once with `setTimeout`. `stop()` clears them, but only
  `store.clear()` calls it, and a second `play` orphans the first's handles (#465).
- **The image cache.** Keyed by file, never evicted (F8). Sprite strips are a bounded set.
- **Sounds.** One `Audio` per file, nothing preloaded: each sound's first play waits for its load.

## Architecture proposals (for the owner)

Each is bigger than a clean-up and changes no behaviour unless it says so. None is done here.

1. **The screen interface in the core's terms.**
   - What: `move(unit: UnitId, to: Position, ...)`, `attack(attacker: UnitId, target: UnitId, ...)` (T6), and the
     AI's actions as a discriminated union (T7).
   - Cost: small. host.ts, nullscreen.ts, src/view/screen.tsx and test/gameturn.test.ts's stand-in change. verify
     should come out identical.
   - Gain: no old object crosses into the UI, and nine casts go.
2. **Shrink the old objects to what the harness and the contracts need.**
   - What: `MapSelection`, `GameMap.currentUnit`, the hexes' sight counters and `CombatLog` are read now only by the
     contracts, the harness and the pinned turns. They could move behind the harness's own module, and the mirror's
     comments be rewritten.
   - Cost: large. The contracts and harness.js read them by name, and test/harness-surface.test.ts guards them. Each
     step needs verify and the contracts identical.
   - Gain: src/ stops carrying the old UI's model; mirror.ts and selection.ts shrink.
3. **Split the store along its seams.**
   - What: a core (world, selected, select, dispatch, refresh); the map; the step; the turn and its reports; the
     unit card; plus the existing menu stores. Components then take the signals they read, not the whole `Store`
     (P1, P5, A15).
   - Cost: about a day, mechanical; touches every .tsx and the view tests.
   - Gain: each part readable and testable alone, smaller render sets, and no name clashes from the spreads.
4. **A reactive settings object.**
   - What: one signal per setting, written through to `uiSettings` for the engine and the saved settings.
   - Cost: small to medium.
   - Gain: removes the three counters and the doubled scale and width (A5), fixes #467 structurally, and gives the
     host the AI's sides as an argument (A11).
   - **Done** in #489: src/view/uisettings.ts puts a signal behind each of `uiSettings`'s keys (the keys redefined in
     place: the engine, the game's state and the harness read and write the object as before, and the settings saved are
     the same JSON). The counters `changed`, `aiChanged` and `saved` went, and the map's scale, the interface's scale and
     width are the settings' own signals. `GameHost.startScenario` takes the AI's sides.
5. **Structural equality for the view's derived objects** (A6).
   - Cost: small.
   - Gain: a step that doesn't touch the selected unit no longer renders the card, the context menu or the reports
     again.
6. **The host's save, load and AI turns without the old UI's shapes.**
   - What: `saveToCloud(id, report)` instead of a fake input (A10, T12); the AI's sides passed to `startScenario`
     (A11); the host playing a program's step and handing the screen the result (A12).
   - Cost: medium; the last one changes how the harness's turn loop sees a program's step, so it needs a verify run.
   - Gain: the host reads like the game's course, not like the old windows.
7. **The canvas's per-frame work** (F1-F6, F8, F9, D11).
   - What: culling to the view first; then a cached bottom layer (terrain, grid, fog), and during beats a cached
     scene under the moving sprite.
   - Cost: culling is small. The layers cost a second canvas the size of the screen (about 8 MB at 1080p, 33 MB at a
     device pixel ratio of 2) and their invalidation on scroll, scale and world.
   - Gain: animation frames from a full redraw (the terrain, about 2,300 hex paths, every unit) to a blit or two.
8. **Accessibility of the windows** (P10-P13).
   - What: focus in and back, disabled reasons reachable, the status line not announcing every hex, the full tab
     roles.
   - Cost: small, but it changes behaviour for keyboard and screen-reader users, so it is a change, played first.
   - Gain: handoff.md's checklist met.
9. **The game's HTML texts from saves** (P15).
   - What: allow-list the tags the scenarios', campaigns' and tutorial's texts use.
   - Cost: small; a security fix with its own issue and test.
   - Gain: a shared save can't run script.
10. **The gallery-only components.**
    - What: src/view/sheet.tsx (D7's bottom sheet), src/view/strength.tsx (the odds card's bar, cut in #441),
      `SegmentedControl`, `Tag` and `Tooltip side="left"` live in src/ for the design gallery only, with their tests
      and coverage floors.
    - Cost: none to keep. Moving them out of src/ means the gallery builds from docs/.
    - Gain: src/ holds what the game runs. Keep them if D7 and the odds card are soon.
